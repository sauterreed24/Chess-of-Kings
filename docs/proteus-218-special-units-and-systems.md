# Proteus 218 Special Units and Depth Systems

This document extends the civilization rework with faction-personalized special units, balance rules, veteran systems, commander identity, map events, and additional systems that make **Proteus 218: River of Crowns** more distinctive and fun without becoming overcomplicated or overpowered.

## Design thesis

Special units should not be generic elite troops. They should be **playable expressions of civilization identity**.

A special unit should answer one of these questions:

- What does this civilization do that no one else can do?
- What does this civilization believe about survival?
- What map problem does this faction solve in a unique way?
- What mistake does this unit tempt the player to make?
- What obvious counterplay does the enemy have?

If a special unit is merely stronger, faster, or tougher, it is not good enough.

## Research-informed principles

The design is inspired by historical patterns without copying real peoples.

- Roman roads show that infrastructure can be command, taxation, supply, occupation, and military movement at once.
- Ancient water systems such as qanats and aflaj show that water infrastructure can be engineering, law, ritual, allocation, and survival.
- Steppe warfare shows that mobility is not just speed. It is scouting, deception, remount endurance, and choosing when battle becomes real.
- Phalanx and shield formations show that heavy infantry is geometry, cohesion, trust, terrain dependence, and vulnerability when disrupted.
- Siege engineering shows that engineers do not simply damage walls. They change where battles can happen.
- Common-pool resource theory gives the game its moral engine: local survival choices can still break the shared world.

## Special unit balance law

Every special unit must have:

1. **A signature moment:** the cool thing players remember.
2. **A doctrine cost:** water, command, civil strain, setup time, terrain dependence, or limited charges.
3. **A tactical counter:** scouting, flanking, terrain denial, siege pressure, supply raids, fatigue, or timing.
4. **A readability cue:** the opponent can tell what is happening.
5. **A non-graphic presentation:** routs, broken formation, dust, banners, damaged gear, exhaustion, or surrender.

Special units should create stories, not stat checks.

## Unit tiers

### 1. Civic Specials

These units interact with cities, roads, waterworks, morale, legitimacy, or conquest aftermath. They make conquest feel deeper.

### 2. Tactical Specials

These units change battle geometry, formation matchups, ambush possibilities, or timing windows.

### 3. Crisis Specials

These units appear or become efficient under drought, siege, collapse, rebellion, or final defense conditions. They create comeback drama without becoming free power.

### 4. Commander Specials

These are not superhero units. They are named command formations or officer circles with aura-like doctrine abilities. They should be vulnerable if isolated.

## Civilization special units

## 1. Auctorate of the Mile-Crown

### Faction verb

**To govern the battlefield.**

The Mile-Crown should make the player feel like roads, forts, laws, and legions are parts of the same weapon.

### Special unit 1: Raven Standard Cohort

**Role:** Command shield infantry.

**Signature moment:** A broken allied line rallies around the black-red raven standard and reforms before the enemy can exploit the gap.

**Ability: Hold the Measure**

The cohort anchors nearby friendly infantry and reduces morale collapse if connected to a road, mile-fort, or tribunal structure.

**Cost:** High command upkeep. Less effective away from governed infrastructure.

**Counterplay:** Cut roads, isolate the cohort, use Lethrynn disruption, or force it to fight in rough terrain.

**Why it is fun:** It turns disciplined recovery into a battlefield identity.

### Special unit 2: Milecutters

**Role:** Engineer-infantry hybrid.

**Signature moment:** A dangerous push suddenly becomes real when Milecutters throw down a rough military road, a palisaded post, and a crossing ramp.

**Ability: Red Road Spur**

Builds temporary road segments and micro-forts faster than normal engineers.

**Cost:** Fragile while building. Temporary roads decay if not connected to stable authority.

**Counterplay:** Raid the build crew, burn the spur, or bait overextension.

**Why it is fun:** The player feels like they are drawing conquest directly onto the map.

### Special unit 3: Third-Line Veterans

**Role:** Reserve rotation infantry.

**Signature moment:** A tired front line withdraws, veterans step through the gap, and the formation survives a decisive enemy push.

**Ability: Rotate the Line**

Swaps with an exhausted friendly infantry formation and restores some cohesion to both units.

**Cost:** Expensive, limited number, and weak if caught alone.

**Counterplay:** Strike them before they rotate, force multi-front battles, or use cavalry to threaten rear lines.

**Why it is fun:** It makes Roman-style disciplined reserves mechanically satisfying.

### Commander special: The Censorial Bench

A mobile legal-command circle that improves annexation and road obedience in conquered cities, but raises civil strain if overused.

## 2. Narethi Silt-Crowns

### Faction verb

**To turn survival into abundance.**

The Silt-Crowns should make water timing feel heroic.

### Special unit 1: Floodglass Archers

**Role:** Elevated precision archers.

**Signature moment:** Enemy infantry climbs a terrace under pressure, only for Floodglass volleys to shatter their cohesion from layered high ground.

**Ability: Sun-on-Water Aim**

Gains accuracy and suppression from terraces, reservoir walls, and silt ridges.

**Cost:** Mediocre on flat open ground. Needs prepared terrain.

**Counterplay:** Force them off elevations, use shields, attack at multiple angles, or sabotage terraces.

**Why it is fun:** It makes terrain preparation visible and rewarding.

### Special unit 2: Cistern Wardens

**Role:** Reservoir guardian infantry.

**Signature moment:** A city almost falls, but Wardens hold the reservoir gate long enough for relief water to arrive.

**Ability: Guard the Lower Measure**

Improves reservoir defense, reduces sabotage damage, and strengthens morale near water infrastructure.

**Cost:** Less useful away from waterworks.

**Counterplay:** Bypass the reservoir, starve roads, or pressure multiple water sites.

**Why it is fun:** It turns infrastructure defense into emotional drama.

### Special unit 3: Orchard Bearers

**Role:** Logistics support.

**Signature moment:** An army that should be thirsty keeps marching because Orchard Bearers preserve water discipline near farms and orchards.

**Ability: Green Ration**

Reduces water consumption for nearby formations when supply lines pass through farms, orchards, or reservoirs.

**Cost:** Vulnerable, slow, and not a combat unit.

**Counterplay:** Cavalry raids, hidden-path ambushes, or scorched farm pressure.

**Why it is fun:** It creates a softer but powerful logistics mini-game.

### Crisis special: Harvest Host

Emergency militia that becomes cheaper after strong harvests or when a reservoir city is attacked. Strong defensively, poor offensively.

## 3. Kharvoden Oathholds

### Faction verb

**To become unmovable.**

The Oathholds should reward pre-battle ground choice and punish reckless pursuit.

### Special unit 1: Black-Pike Oathline

**Role:** Elite anchored spear formation.

**Signature moment:** Cavalry and infantry crash into a planted pike line and simply cannot move it.

**Ability: Plant the Black Pike**

Locks into oath-stance, gaining major frontal defense and morale pressure when anchored to terrain.

**Cost:** Slow to enter and exit. Vulnerable to flanks, missiles, and forced repositioning.

**Counterplay:** Refuse the front, flank, use engineers to alter terrain, or bait them off anchor points.

**Why it is fun:** It makes “hold this ground” feel like a sacred tactical decision.

### Special unit 2: Pass Ravens

**Role:** Mountain scouts and anti-ambush unit.

**Signature moment:** The enemy tries a hidden pass maneuver, but Pass Ravens reveal it early and the Oathline turns to meet them.

**Ability: Read the Stone Wind**

Reveals movement through ridges, ravines, and pass mouths.

**Cost:** Weak in combat and less useful in open plains.

**Counterplay:** False paths, decoys, and multi-route pressure.

**Why it is fun:** It lets a defensive faction feel intelligent instead of passive.

### Special unit 3: Iron Exiles

**Role:** Political shock infantry.

**Signature moment:** Disgraced warriors redeem themselves by breaking a tired enemy line.

**Ability: Debt of Stone**

Deals high cohesion damage to exhausted or wavering formations.

**Cost:** Raises internal strain if too many are fielded. Weak against fresh spears and missiles.

**Counterplay:** Keep lines rested, avoid exhausted engagements, or focus them with archers.

**Why it is fun:** It gives the faction a risky offensive finisher.

### Commander special: Moot-Bearer Guard

A command formation that strengthens nearby anchored units but loses value when the army is forced to move repeatedly.

## 4. Kharuun Moving Court

### Faction verb

**To make the enemy fight a horizon.**

The Moving Court should be about tempo, false information, wells, and choosing the actual battle.

### Special unit 1: Dust Doubles

**Role:** Deception cavalry.

**Signature moment:** The enemy rotates an army to answer a threat that was never real.

**Ability: Raise a False Column**

Creates a visible decoy movement report or dust trail that can mislead unscouted enemies.

**Cost:** No real combat power. Fails against disciplined scouts and watchtowers.

**Counterplay:** Scout confirmation, Mile-Crown watch roads, Pass Ravens, or Silent Observatories.

**Why it is fun:** It gives the player a legal mind-game without unfair invisibility.

### Special unit 2: Moon-Bow Riders

**Role:** Mounted harassment archers.

**Signature moment:** They circle, fire, withdraw, and leave a heavy formation too angry and tired to fight well.

**Ability: Cant of the Turning Saddle**

Can fire during controlled withdrawal and inflict fatigue on pursuing enemies.

**Cost:** Poor against fortified archers, spear zones, and rough terrain.

**Counterplay:** Do not chase blindly, use shielded formations, trap with barriers, or deny wells.

**Why it is fun:** It transforms “hit and run” into a readable tactical dance.

### Special unit 3: Well-Takers

**Role:** Water objective raiders.

**Signature moment:** A quiet raid flips a well, and an enemy army suddenly cannot stay in the field.

**Ability: Claim the Throat**

Captures, drains, sabotages, or marks wells faster than normal infantry.

**Cost:** Fragile and poor in open battle.

**Counterplay:** Guard wells, build cistern vaults, patrol with light cavalry.

**Why it is fun:** It makes water points tactically explosive.

### Commander special: Oath Singers

Mounted morale commanders that keep forces coherent during long maneuvers. If killed or routed, nearby clan units lose confidence faster.

## 5. Sluice Synod of Veyr

### Faction verb

**To author terrain.**

The Synod should feel like the faction that changes the shape of the war.

### Special unit 1: Gatewright Cadres

**Role:** Elite engineer-command unit.

**Signature moment:** A useless ford becomes a lethal controlled crossing because Gatewrights prepared it three minutes earlier.

**Ability: Draft the Field**

Builds advanced temporary fieldworks: crossing chains, road locks, pump lines, and counter-siege platforms.

**Cost:** Expensive, slow setup, vulnerable while working.

**Counterplay:** Scout early, raid engineers, attack before works finish, or force fights elsewhere.

**Why it is fun:** It gives the player the feeling of outsmarting the map itself.

### Special unit 2: Chain Wardens

**Role:** Anti-cavalry barrier infantry.

**Signature moment:** Cavalry charge lanes close at the last second and the enemy’s tempo dies.

**Ability: Drop the Chain**

Deploys temporary anti-cavalry barriers across roads, fords, and narrow spaces.

**Cost:** Low mobility while deployed, weak to missiles and siege.

**Counterplay:** Bait deployment, attack from range, flank through open ground.

**Why it is fun:** It creates tactical “trapdoor” moments without instant killing.

### Special unit 3: Counterweight Crews

**Role:** Siege and structure pressure.

**Signature moment:** The enemy’s proud wall becomes a liability because the Synod has measured it.

**Ability: Measured Break**

After setup, deals focused structural pressure and reveals weak points in fortifications.

**Cost:** Setup time, protection required, poor against mobile enemies.

**Counterplay:** Raid, relocate, sortie, or disrupt with hidden units.

**Why it is fun:** It gives siege a puzzle-like payoff.

### Commander special: The Measure Hall Delegation

A slow command group that reduces build waste and speeds fieldwork planning near a front, but becomes a high-value target.

## 6. Lethrynn Veilways

### Faction verb

**To fight from the missing part of the map.**

The Veilways should control uncertainty, but never in a way that feels cheap.

### Special unit 1: Reedghosts

**Role:** Ambush skirmishers.

**Signature moment:** A supply train enters reeds, horns go silent, and the enemy’s siege push stalls.

**Ability: Strike from the Uncounted Path**

Gains opening ambush bonuses from cover, hidden paths, ravines, and dry streambeds.

**Cost:** Weak in prolonged combat and exposed terrain.

**Counterplay:** Scouts, watch posts, road patrols, and clearing cover.

**Why it is fun:** It makes terrain memory feel like a weapon.

### Special unit 2: Hush Drummers

**Role:** Command disruption support.

**Signature moment:** The enemy tries to issue a clean retreat or formation change, but their orders lag inside Veilway country.

**Ability: Muffle the Command Horns**

In forests and ravines, increases enemy command delay for nearby formations.

**Cost:** No direct damage, fragile, terrain dependent.

**Counterplay:** Kill support units, leave cover, use disciplined command units.

**Why it is fun:** It attacks the enemy’s timing rather than their health.

### Special unit 3: Root-Pathers

**Role:** Hidden-route scouts.

**Signature moment:** A small army appears behind a siege camp through a path the opponent did not know existed.

**Ability: Remember the Child-Map**

Reveals hidden movement lanes and improves friendly movement through local terrain.

**Cost:** Must scout first. Weak in open combat.

**Counterplay:** Deny scouting, build watch roads, use Pass Ravens or Observatories.

**Why it is fun:** It makes discovery itself part of the faction’s power curve.

### Crisis special: Green Oath Spears

Local defensive spears that become stronger when defending hamlets, shrines, or hidden paths, but cannot project power far from home.

## 7. Ash-Salt Ledger

### Faction verb

**To survive the impossible bill.**

The Ledger should be harsh, memorable, and morally uncomfortable without becoming villainous.

### Special unit 1: Ration Judges

**Role:** Scarcity command unit.

**Signature moment:** A doomed army survives one more march because the Judges counted every cup.

**Ability: Last Measures**

Reduces water consumption and fatigue for nearby units or cities under ration law.

**Cost:** Increases civil strain and slows growth if used too long.

**Counterplay:** Force repeated ration cycles, trigger unrest, or strike the Judges.

**Why it is fun:** It gives the faction power with visible moral and strategic debt.

### Special unit 2: Cistern Guard

**Role:** Defensive water-store infantry.

**Signature moment:** Raiders reach the vault, but the Guard holds long enough to prevent a campaign collapse.

**Ability: Seal the Vault**

Protects water stores from sabotage and reduces siege water loss.

**Cost:** Poor offensive value.

**Counterplay:** Bypass vaults, attack markets, or force them to fight away from stored water.

**Why it is fun:** It makes water defense feel tense.

### Special unit 3: Ledger Riders

**Role:** Supply-raiding light cavalry.

**Signature moment:** The enemy wins a field battle, then realizes its supply line has been audited into ruin.

**Ability: Mark the Debt**

Targets supply routes and increases enemy upkeep/fatigue if the route remains contested.

**Cost:** Fragile against prepared cavalry, forts, and archers.

**Counterplay:** Escort routes, build mile-forts, or use mobile scouts.

**Why it is fun:** It lets the Ledger win ugly without feeling random.

### Commander special: The Witness Cart

A slow, guarded mobile record shrine that reduces unrest from rationing when protected, but causes a morale shock if captured.

## 8. Orrery Academies of Ithra

### Faction verb

**To learn the enemy into defeat.**

Ithra should feel like the game is watching patterns and rewarding active study.

### Special unit 1: Archive Scouts

**Role:** Doctrine intelligence.

**Signature moment:** The player sees the enemy’s repeated cavalry pattern become readable and counterable.

**Ability: Read the March**

Collects doctrine data from enemy units, routes, and structures.

**Cost:** Weak combat value and requires active scouting.

**Counterplay:** Kill scouts, use decoys, vary composition, deny vision.

**Why it is fun:** It turns scouting into progression.

### Special unit 2: Mirror Guard

**Role:** Adaptive infantry.

**Signature moment:** A unit shifts stance before battle, becoming the exact kind of answer the player earned through observation.

**Ability: Change the Proof**

Can shift between shield, spear, and sword emphasis after doctrine knowledge is unlocked.

**Cost:** Cooldown, moderate stats, vulnerable during transition.

**Counterplay:** Force frequent stance changes, attack during cooldown, use mixed threats.

**Why it is fun:** It gives a high-skill faction a tangible payoff.

### Special unit 3: Null Tutors

**Role:** Anti-commander support.

**Signature moment:** An enemy commander’s aura fades because Ithra has studied the command pattern.

**Ability: Annotate the Habit**

Reduces enemy commander bonuses after enough observation time.

**Cost:** No direct damage, fragile, requires prior scouting.

**Counterplay:** Kill them, rotate commanders, change doctrine, deny observation.

**Why it is fun:** It makes enemy habits feel exploitable.

### Commander special: Orrery Cadres

Command-scholars who accelerate doctrine learning near battles. Powerful in long games, dangerous to expose early.

## Cross-faction special unit interactions

These interactions should be designed deliberately:

- **Milecutters vs Reedghosts:** road building reveals hidden routes but creates ambushable work crews.
- **Floodglass Archers vs Black-Pike Oathline:** archers punish anchored infantry if given elevation; Oathline wins if it reaches protected ground.
- **Dust Doubles vs Archive Scouts:** deception works until Ithra has enough doctrine data.
- **Gatewright Cadres vs Kharuun Moving Court:** Veyr can trap mobility only if it predicts the route before Kharuun shifts.
- **Ration Judges vs Narethi Relief Gates:** ration discipline beats drought over time, but stored abundance can create sudden tempo reversals.
- **Hush Drummers vs Raven Standard Cohort:** Veilways delay orders; Mile-Crown restores order if infrastructure is intact.

## Other elements that make the game better

## 1. Named formations, not superhero heroes

Instead of single overpowered hero units, use named formations and officer circles:

- Raven Standard Cohort
- The Witness Cart
- Orrery Cadres
- Censorial Bench
- Moot-Bearer Guard

This keeps personality without creating one-unit dominance.

## 2. Veteran epithets

Units that survive meaningful battles can earn epithets. These are small, flavorful modifiers, not huge upgrades.

Examples:

- **Ford-Hardened:** less morale loss when crossing rivers.
- **Dustwise:** better resistance to deception.
- **Gate-Burned:** bonus against Veyr fieldworks.
- **Oath-Steady:** reduced panic when flanked once per battle.
- **Reservoir-Blessed:** consumes slightly less water near allied waterworks.

This creates emotional attachment and replay stories.

## 3. Doctrine scars

Civilizations remember how they were hurt.

If a faction loses many units to cavalry raids, it can unlock an anti-raid doctrine scar. If it repeatedly loses reservoirs, it can develop harsher water law. This creates campaign continuity without requiring a complex diplomacy system.

## 4. Battlefield vows

Before major campaign battles, the player can choose one vow:

- **Hold the Gate:** bonuses near objective, penalty when abandoning it.
- **No Wasteful March:** lower supply cost, slower pursuit.
- **Break Their Road:** bonuses against infrastructure, lower city integration.
- **Spare the Granaries:** better post-conquest legitimacy, slower siege pressure.

These are short-term strategic promises with tradeoffs.

## 5. Conquest aftermath choices

After total victory over a city, avoid diplomacy systems but keep conquest meaningful:

- **Annex:** balanced long-term value, slow integration.
- **Protect:** lower unrest, lower extraction.
- **Militarize:** faster recruitment, higher civil strain.
- **Strip:** immediate resources, long-term hatred and lower productivity.
- **Oath-settle:** faction-specific integration method.

This gives conquering depth without turning the game into a negotiation simulator.

## 6. Drought clock phases

Every match has a visible drought clock:

1. **Green Memory:** normal economy, faster expansion.
2. **Low Gauge:** water routes matter.
3. **Dust Tax:** armies far from supply fatigue faster.
4. **Gate Panic:** water infrastructure becomes prime target.
5. **Last Current:** total-war tempo accelerates; holding water is victory momentum.

This prevents stale late games.

## 7. Commander temperament

Each faction can choose one commander temperament before duel mode:

- **Measured:** safer economy, slower aggression.
- **Severe:** stronger crisis powers, more civil strain.
- **Restless:** faster scouting and raids, weaker defense.
- **Architect:** better fieldworks and city planning, slower army mass.
- **Merciful:** better conquered-city stability, weaker extraction.

This adds replayability without creating too many civilizations.

## 8. Map memory

The map should not feel fully known after one match. Civilizations should reveal different truths:

- Mile-Crown sees road potential.
- Silt-Crowns see water storage potential.
- Oathholds see anchor points.
- Moving Court sees wells and false horizons.
- Synod sees buildable chokepoints.
- Veilways see hidden paths.
- Ledger sees ration routes.
- Ithra sees observation value.

Same map, different mental game.

## 9. Anti-staleness events

Events should be system-driven, not random nonsense:

- A tributary fails.
- A well deepens after investment.
- A road riot breaks out after overextension.
- A hidden path is revealed by drought.
- A gate jams and must be repaired or exploited.
- Refugees appear and can be protected, taxed, relocated, or ignored.

Events should create decisions, not just punishment.

## 10. Duel mode special rules

Duel mode should allow optional modifiers:

- **Seven Gates:** more gate objectives and crossings.
- **Dry Interior:** more wells and cavalry value.
- **Old Road War:** prebuilt roads become major objectives.
- **Hidden Country:** more cover, ravines, and path uncertainty.
- **Iron Spring:** mountain wells and chokepoints matter more.
- **Last Current:** drought clock starts later but accelerates harder.

These increase replayability while staying land-only.

## Special unit UI requirements

Every special unit card should show:

- **What it does** in one sentence.
- **When it shines.**
- **What it costs.**
- **How enemies counter it.**
- **Best paired with.**
- **Bad habit it tempts.**

Example:

**Moon-Bow Riders**

- Does: harass and fatigue pursuers during controlled retreats.
- Shines: open ground, well chains, false retreats.
- Costs: weak direct combat, poor in rough terrain.
- Countered by: spears, forts, scouts, disciplined non-pursuit.
- Best paired with: Dust Doubles and Well-Takers.
- Bad habit: over-kiting instead of capturing objectives.

## Balance tests Codex should add

1. Every civilization has at least three special units and one commander/crisis special.
2. Every special unit has counterplay.
3. Every special unit has a doctrine cost.
4. No special unit has more than two primary strengths.
5. No civilization has specials covering all weaknesses.
6. Every special unit card can be rendered from data.
7. Every special unit can be disabled or tuned independently.
8. Special unit names are unique.
9. Special unit abilities never create instant unavoidable wins.
10. All unit data validates without network access.

## Final standard

A player should talk about these units the way chess players talk about pieces and openings:

- “I lost because I chased Moon-Bows past the wells.”
- “I should have killed the Gatewrights before the crossing locked.”
- “The Raven Standard saved his center, so I cut the road instead.”
- “I thought the Veilway army was retreating, but it was a child-map path.”
- “I let Ithra watch me too long.”

That is the goal: personalized faction units that create memorable strategic stories without becoming overpowered shortcuts.

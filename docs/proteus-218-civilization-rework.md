# Proteus 218 Civilization Rework

Research-informed naming, backstory, strategy, and fun pass for **Proteus 218: River of Crowns**.

This document replaces the first-pass faction names with stronger player-facing civilization identities. The goal is to make each faction feel original, playable, strategically memorable, lore-rich, and mechanically testable.

## Research transformation

The new faction design borrows patterns from history without copying historical peoples.

- Roman roads and military engineering inspire the idea that infrastructure is not passive. Roads can be law, tax, supply, reinforcement, occupation, and vulnerability.
- Ancient hydraulic systems inspire the idea that water is not a resource node. Water is governance, ritual, engineering, legitimacy, and war tempo.
- Steppe warfare inspires the idea that mobility is not just fast movement. It is scouting, false absence, decoy commitment, multi-horse endurance, and choosing the battlefield.
- Phalanx and shield-wall warfare inspire the idea that heavy formations are not just high armor. They are geometry, cohesion, trust, terrain dependence, and morale.
- Siege warfare inspires the idea that engineers do not just break walls. They author the map.
- Common-pool resource theory inspires the central tragedy: rational local survival can still destroy the shared river system.

## Naming rule

Each civilization has three names:

1. **Player-facing name:** memorable, cool, selectable.
2. **Endonym:** what the civilization calls itself.
3. **Enemy nickname:** what frightened opponents call it.

This lets the UI stay readable while the lore feels deep.

## The faction design formula

Each faction should be built from five linked pieces:

**Homeland pressure + survival thesis + fun spike + civil cost + counterplay.**

If a faction cannot be explained through that formula, it is not ready.

## 1. Auctorate of the Mile-Crown

### Core fantasy

You are not Rome. You are what Rome becomes when water fails and every road becomes a verdict.

The Auctorate was born from the old Concord road service. Its magistrates maintained milestones, relays, bridges, census tablets, fort granaries, and flood calendars. When the drought begins, they are the only faction capable of moving food quickly across multiple provinces. They see themselves as the last grown-up institution on Proteus 218.

Other civilizations see a different truth: every relief convoy arrives with soldiers, every bridge repair becomes a checkpoint, and every emergency road becomes a permanent claim.

### Survival thesis

**Order must move faster than famine.**

### Strategic identity

Beginner friendly, but deep at mastery. The Auctorate wins by building a living road-web that converts territory into reliable supply, quick reinforcement, tax order, and disciplined army recovery.

They do not have the fastest cavalry, highest food, strongest siege, or sneakiest tactics. They win by making the map increasingly legible and increasingly theirs.

### Signature mechanic: The Road Has Teeth

Roads are not just movement bonuses. A connected Auctorate road network creates:

- reinforcement lanes
- tax stability
- marching stamina
- faster retreat recovery
- fort vision
- city obedience pressure
- emergency relief movement

But overextended roads create liability. Raids, sabotage, and unrest can turn roads into panic lines. The farther the road is from secure authority, the more expensive it is to hold.

### Fun spike

**Lay the Red Road:** for a short period, engineers and cohorts build a forced-march road and mile-fort chain toward a target. It feels incredible when used to crack open a campaign front, but it becomes a dangerous exposed artery if the push fails.

### Unique units

- **Raven Standard Cohort:** disciplined shield infantry that rallies nearby routed units and gains bonuses on road-influence tiles.
- **Milecutters:** engineer infantry that rapidly builds roads, mile-forts, and fortified ramps.
- **Bridge-Lictors:** spear-shield guards that hold crossings and reduce unrest in captured bridge towns.
- **Third-Line Veterans:** expensive reserve infantry that can rotate into exhausted formations and restore line cohesion.
- **Causeway Equites:** medium cavalry that redeploys quickly along controlled roads but is weaker in rough or hidden terrain.

### Unique structures

- **Mile-Fort:** small fortified node that extends supply and vision.
- **Census Granary:** stores food, stabilizes drought shock, and improves annexation.
- **Tribunal Bridge:** fortified crossing that converts control of river chokepoints into legal authority.
- **Relay Shrine:** morale and command post that speeds orders along connected roads.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** secure roads, build small forts, pressure exposed villages.
- **Medium:** create a connected road triangle between three cities, then push with layered cohorts.
- **Long:** make rebellion and raiding mathematically impossible by turning the map into a governed network.

### Mistake that loses games

Building roads faster than you can defend them. The Auctorate should feel powerful, but not effortless.

### Best counters

Kharuun raids, Lethrynn sabotage, Ash-Salt attrition, and Veyr gate-locks.

### Campaign tragedy

They are the peacekeepers who become occupiers because emergency authority never finds a clean stopping point.

## 2. Narethi Silt-Crowns

### Core fantasy

You are the downstream civilization that can feed the world if the world stops strangling your water.

The Narethi live in lower river terraces where the Aurelian slows, widens, and lays down silt. Their cities are ringed with orchard walls, reservoir courts, stepped farms, and public grain theaters where harvest oaths are recited aloud. They are beloved by the poor and feared by upstream rulers because their moral claim is obvious: people should eat before kings count glory.

The drought makes them desperate. A locked upstream gate can destroy a perfect Narethi season.

### Survival thesis

**A city is judged by who survives its hungriest year.**

### Strategic identity

Defensive economic powerhouse. Narethi play is about banking water, choosing when to spend it, and making the enemy attack into prepared abundance.

They are not passive turtle farmers. They create huge comeback moments when reservoirs are opened at the perfect time.

### Signature mechanic: The Lower Measure

Reservoirs store water across drought phases. Stored water can be spent to:

- prevent crop failure
- speed emergency militia
- heal city unrest
- support distant armies
- increase archer readiness on terraces
- keep siege defenders supplied

If reservoirs are captured, poisoned, drained, or cut off, the Narethi collapse harder than most factions.

### Fun spike

**Open the Relief Gates:** spend stored water to instantly stabilize a city, raise Flood Levy units, and restore terrace production. Used well, it turns a doomed defense into a heroic counterattack.

### Unique units

- **Floodglass Archers:** elevated archers who gain accuracy on terraces, walls, and silt ridges.
- **Cistern Wardens:** spear-shield guards that improve reservoir defense and city morale.
- **Canal Knives:** fast repair and sabotage infantry that move quickly through irrigated terrain.
- **Harvest Host:** seasonal militia that becomes cheaper after strong harvest years.
- **Orchard Bearers:** support unit that reduces army water consumption when near farms and reservoirs.

### Unique structures

- **Step Reservoir:** stores water and powers Narethi economy.
- **Silt Theatre:** civic morale structure that reduces panic during drought.
- **Terrace Wall:** farm-defense hybrid that buffs archers and slows cavalry.
- **Relief Hall:** reduces famine unrest in conquered cities but costs stored water.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** protect reservoir sites and terrace farms.
- **Medium:** bait attacks into prepared terrace zones, then counterattack with stored water.
- **Long:** outproduce enemies and turn relief policy into conquest legitimacy.

### Mistake that loses games

Spending reservoir water too early. Narethi fun comes from timing, not hoarding forever.

### Best counters

Veyr gate control, Kharuun well raids, Mile-Crown legal encirclement, and Ash-Salt ration pressure.

### Campaign tragedy

They are morally right that people need food. They are strategically trapped because fairness downstream requires force upstream.

## 3. Kharvoden Oathholds

### Core fantasy

You are the highland people no empire can digest.

The Kharvoden live in spring-fed valleys, iron ridges, frost basins, and fortified canton halls. Their warriors swear stone-oaths to hold passes, wells, and ancestor roads. They distrust river capitals because river capitals always eventually call mountain independence a logistical inconvenience.

They do not want empire. The drought makes their springs and iron too valuable for neutrality.

### Survival thesis

**A people that cannot be moved cannot be erased.**

### Strategic identity

Terrain-anchored formation mastery. Kharvoden armies are slow, disciplined, and frighteningly strong when deployed correctly. They are the anti-spam faction. Positioning is everything.

### Signature mechanic: Stone-Oath Anchors

Kharvoden formations gain power when anchored to:

- ridges
- walls
- cliffs
- spring houses
- allied shield lines
- fortified stakes
- pass mouths

Anchored units resist morale shock and frontal attacks. Moving breaks the oath temporarily, making bad redeployment costly.

### Fun spike

**Plant the Black Pike:** a formation locks into an oath-stance, becoming brutally hard to break from the front while slowly generating morale pressure on enemies facing it.

### Unique units

- **Black-Pike Oathline:** elite spear formation with devastating frontal denial when anchored.
- **Springward Shields:** defensive infantry that protect wells and restore nearby morale.
- **Iron Exiles:** shock infantry made from dishonored oathbreakers seeking restoration. Powerful, but increases political strain if overused.
- **Pass Ravens:** mountain scouts that reveal ambushes and enemy approach routes.
- **Hammer Cantors:** slow heavy infantry that break exhausted shield lines.

### Unique structures

- **Oathhold:** compact fortress-city that boosts nearby militia.
- **Spring House:** protects water and reduces drought penalties.
- **Cliff Signal:** early warning tower in mountain terrain.
- **Iron Moot:** upgrades heavy infantry but increases elite political influence.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** hold springs and passes.
- **Medium:** force enemies into bad terrain and punish frontal arrogance.
- **Long:** become the iron spine of the war, controlling the materials everyone needs.

### Mistake that loses games

Chasing mobile enemies into open ground. Kharvoden players win by making enemies come to the wrong place.

### Best counters

Lethrynn false paths, Veyr route engineering, Kharuun refusal to engage, and Ithrian doctrine counters.

### Campaign tragedy

Their neutrality is principled, but every neutral spring becomes a battlefield once the river order fails.

## 4. Kharuun Moving Court

### Core fantasy

You are the civilization that refuses to become a target.

The Kharuun are riders, caravan judges, herd keepers, oath poets, and mobile magistrates of the dry interior. Outsiders call them raiders because outsiders only notice them when they arrive. The Kharuun remember a time when interior grasslands fed millions. Now the wells are failing, and the fixed river states are pretending the disaster stops at their borders.

Their capital is not a city. It is wherever the court has water, horses, and witnesses.

### Survival thesis

**The horizon is a wall only fools cannot see.**

### Strategic identity

Advanced tempo faction. The Kharuun win by scouting, misdirecting, starving supply, isolating armies, and choosing the decisive fight.

They are not just fast. They are strategically slippery.

### Signature mechanic: Court Beneath No Roof

Kharuun mobile courts act as moving bases. They can relocate, train light units, resupply cavalry, and project command. They can create decoy camps to mislead enemies.

But if pinned against fortified terrain, their advantages collapse.

### Fun spike

**False Horizon:** a selected cavalry force performs a controlled retreat, leaving a misleading vulnerability. If the enemy pursues too far, hidden riders gain a massive encirclement and morale bonus.

### Unique units

- **Moon-Bow Riders:** mobile missile cavalry that harass and disengage.
- **Well-Takers:** light infantry that capture, drain, or sabotage wells quickly.
- **Kharu Lancers:** medium cavalry that devastate disordered units.
- **Oath Singers:** mounted command unit that keeps morale high during long maneuvers.
- **Dust Doubles:** decoy riders that create false movement reports.

### Unique structures

- **Moving Court:** relocatable command camp.
- **Horse Lines:** cavalry recovery and remount hub.
- **Signal Mound:** temporary vision node.
- **Witness Circle:** legitimizes captured wells and reduces clan fragmentation.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** scout, raid, deny wells, never fight fair.
- **Medium:** split enemy armies and collapse their logistics.
- **Long:** turn mobility into conquest by chaining wells and mobile courts.

### Mistake that loses games

Thinking speed alone wins. Kharuun require scouting discipline and timing.

### Best counters

Kharvoden spear anchors, Mile-Crown forts, Veyr barriers, and Narethi prepared terraces.

### Campaign tragedy

They are introduced as raiders, then revealed as climate refugees with a legal tradition the river empires never bothered to learn.

## 5. Sluice Synod of Veyr

### Core fantasy

You are the faction that can rewrite the battlefield.

The Synod of Veyr descends from the ancient gatekeepers who maintained the Seven Gates of the Aurelian. They know flow rates, stone stress, sediment patterns, lock timing, bridge load, cistern depth, and siege angles. They believe the Concord failed because politicians argued while water escaped.

Their critics call them tyrants. Their answer is simple: a wrong vote cannot refill a river.

### Survival thesis

**Water obeys design before it obeys mercy.**

### Strategic identity

Engineer-control faction. Veyr wins by turning neutral terrain into authored terrain: chokepoints, locks, blocked roads, forced crossings, siege lanes, and fortified pump lines.

### Signature mechanic: Works of Necessity

Veyr engineers can build advanced fieldworks that alter tactical geometry:

- road locks
- temporary walls
- crossing chains
- pump towers
- counter-siege platforms
- sediment traps
- forced ford markers

They are terrifying when prepared and vulnerable while preparing.

### Fun spike

**Raise the Murder-Gate:** lock a crossing or road-node briefly, trapping enemy formations inside a prepared kill zone. It feels brilliant when earned, but it requires setup and can be scouted.

### Unique units

- **Gatewright Cadres:** elite engineers that build and repair advanced works.
- **Siphon Shields:** heavy infantry trained to hold waterworks.
- **Chain Wardens:** anti-cavalry infantry that deploy temporary barriers.
- **Counterweight Crews:** siege teams that set up slowly but crush structures.
- **Levelers:** surveyor scouts that reveal terrain-control opportunities.

### Unique structures

- **Sluice Lock:** controls movement and water flow around crossings.
- **Pump Tower:** redistributes local water and supports sieges.
- **Counterweight Yard:** improves siege equipment and fieldworks.
- **Measure Hall:** reduces construction waste and improves engineer planning.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** secure key crossings and build safe works.
- **Medium:** force enemies to fight through engineered geometry.
- **Long:** control the Seven Gates and make all conquest routes pass through Veyr decisions.

### Mistake that loses games

Overbuilding before fielding enough soldiers. Veyr must survive long enough for the map to become theirs.

### Best counters

Kharuun early raids, Lethrynn sabotage, Ithrian counter-analysis, and Mile-Crown road pressure.

### Campaign tragedy

They might be right about the water system and still wrong about human beings.

## 6. Lethrynn Veilways

### Core fantasy

You are the people the map forgot because the map was made by conquerors.

The Lethrynn live in reed corridors, gallery forests, ravines, dry streambeds, hidden hamlets, and shaded tributary paths. They maintain oral maps that do not look like maps: songs, knots, carved reeds, moon calendars, and child games.

Empires call their land a corridor. They call it home.

### Survival thesis

**A path known only to the hunted is stronger than a wall.**

### Strategic identity

Ambush and information-control faction. Lethrynn play is about making the enemy uncertain without making the player confused. They reveal hidden paths, plant false routes, disrupt supply, and punish careless pursuit.

### Signature mechanic: The Map Lies

The Lethrynn can create false scouting impressions and use hidden ways in mapped terrain. Enemy players may see phantom trails, inaccurate army sizes, or delayed movement reports if they lack proper scouts.

This must remain fair. The UI should warn the opponent when they are in Veilway country.

### Fun spike

**Uncounted Path:** reposition a small force through hidden terrain and strike an exposed supply route, siege crew, or retreating army.

### Unique units

- **Reedghosts:** stealth skirmishers that ambush from cover.
- **Thorn-Slingers:** missile troops that slow enemy formations and punish pursuit.
- **Root-Pathers:** scouts that reveal hidden ways and detect traps.
- **Green Oath Spears:** light spears that become strong in cover or defensive terrain.
- **Hush Drummers:** support units that delay enemy command response in forests and ravines.

### Unique structures

- **Hidden Hamlet:** low-visibility economy node.
- **Reed Shrine:** morale and recovery site tied to local legitimacy.
- **False Ford Marker:** misleads enemy pathing unless scouted.
- **Trail Snare:** non-graphic disruption trap that damages cohesion, not bodies.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** scout and mislead.
- **Medium:** bleed supply and punish siege attempts.
- **Long:** turn the enemy empire into isolated pockets that cannot safely move.

### Mistake that loses games

Trying to fight like a normal army in open ground.

### Best counters

Mile-Crown watch roads, Veyr route locks, Kharvoden disciplined scouts, and Ash-Salt attrition resistance.

### Campaign tragedy

To preserve a hidden culture, they must become visible through war.

## 7. Ash-Salt Ledger

### Core fantasy

You are the civilization that planned for the year everyone else called impossible.

The Ash-Salt Ledger comes from salt flats, mineral badlands, dry canyons, caravan vaults, and cistern markets. Their laws are harsh because their homeland punishes waste brutally. They are not greedy caricatures. They are scarcity realists.

Other factions hate hearing a Ledger judge say, “Mercy without arithmetic is murder deferred.”

### Survival thesis

**Every cup has a witness.**

### Strategic identity

Attrition, rationing, and endurance. The Ledger wins by making enemy plans too expensive to continue. They do not always win battles beautifully. They win campaigns by refusing to break.

### Signature mechanic: Last Measures

The Ledger can impose ration laws on armies and cities:

- reduce water consumption
- extend siege endurance
- prevent drought collapse
- increase resource extraction
- stabilize desert routes

Overuse creates unrest, reduced growth, and legitimacy damage.

### Fun spike

**Count the Last Cup:** for a limited time, an army or city becomes brutally efficient under ration law, surviving a crisis that would break anyone else. Afterward, civil strain must be paid down.

### Unique units

- **Salt Wardens:** medium infantry with low supply consumption and high endurance.
- **Ledger Riders:** light cavalry that raid supply and enforce orders.
- **Canyon Bows:** archers with bonuses in badlands and narrow passes.
- **Ration Judges:** command unit that reduces supply use but raises unrest if stationed too long.
- **Cistern Guard:** defensive infantry that protect water stores and resist siege fatigue.

### Unique structures

- **Cistern Vault:** protects stored water from raids and drought loss.
- **Salt Market:** converts mineral wealth into supply efficiency.
- **Debt Court:** extracts more from captured cities at the cost of rebellion risk.
- **Ash Granary:** stores poor-quality emergency food that prevents collapse but lowers morale.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** survive and punish overextension.
- **Medium:** force enemies into bad supply trades.
- **Long:** make every enemy offensive collapse under its own cost.

### Mistake that loses games

Treating ration law as free power. The Ledger is strongest when harshness is timed, not spammed.

### Best counters

Narethi abundance, Kharuun market raids, Ithrian adaptation, and decisive Mile-Crown road pushes.

### Campaign tragedy

They look cruel because they price scarcity aloud, but they saw the catastrophe earlier than everyone else.

## 8. Orrery Academies of Ithra

### Core fantasy

You are the civilization that turns enemy habit into a weapon.

The Academies of Ithra are observatories, war colleges, archive courts, memory gardens, and proof fields. Their scholars once trained officers for every major civilization. During the drought, they fracture between archivists who want neutrality and war theorists who believe neutral knowledge will be destroyed by the first empire that wins.

They do not begin strong. They become terrifying when they understand the opponent.

### Survival thesis

**A war understood too late is still a war lost.**

### Strategic identity

Expert adaptive faction. Ithra wins by scouting, observing, decoding enemy doctrine, and unlocking counter-proofs.

### Signature mechanic: Proof of the Enemy

Ithra gains doctrine knowledge from:

- scouting unit compositions
- surviving battles
- observing construction choices
- reading supply routes
- losing specific unit types
- capturing archives or officers

Knowledge unlocks temporary counter-doctrines, not permanent raw stat superiority.

### Fun spike

**Solved Doctrine:** after enough observation, Ithra declares a counter-proof against one enemy pattern, such as cavalry raids, shield walls, siege works, or reservoir defense. For a short window, the player gets a tactical answer that feels earned.

### Unique units

- **Mirror Guard:** flexible infantry that can shift stance between shield, spear, and sword emphasis.
- **Archive Scouts:** scouts that collect doctrine data faster.
- **Proof Engines:** light engineer-siege crews that expose fortification weaknesses.
- **Orrery Cadres:** command scholars that accelerate adaptation near battles.
- **Null Tutors:** support units that reduce enemy commander bonuses after observation.

### Unique structures

- **War Orrery:** tracks enemy doctrine and unlocks counter-proofs.
- **Archive Court:** stores discovered knowledge and preserves campaign codex entries.
- **Trial Field:** trains veteran traits and tests unit counters.
- **Silent Observatory:** improves scouting range and enemy movement prediction.

### Short, medium, long game

- **Short:** survive and observe.
- **Medium:** counter the enemy’s favorite plan.
- **Long:** become the only faction whose army feels custom-built for the war that actually happened.

### Mistake that loses games

Waiting passively. Ithra must scout aggressively and learn under pressure.

### Best counters

Kharuun early raids, Mile-Crown scouting denial, Veyr forced geometry, and Ash-Salt slow strangulation.

### Campaign tragedy

They understand why everyone is wrong, but understanding does not automatically feed cities.

## Why these names are stronger

The original names sounded like plausible placeholders. The new names carry playable identity.

- **Auctorate of the Mile-Crown** immediately suggests law, roads, hierarchy, and Roman crisis authority.
- **Narethi Silt-Crowns** suggests downstream agriculture, beauty, dependence, and legitimacy through food.
- **Kharvoden Oathholds** suggests stone, iron, vows, and unmovable defense.
- **Kharuun Moving Court** suggests mobility with legal seriousness, not generic nomads.
- **Sluice Synod of Veyr** suggests water engineering, technocracy, and gate power.
- **Lethrynn Veilways** suggests hidden movement and a culture that survives off-map.
- **Ash-Salt Ledger** suggests ration law, harsh arithmetic, and desert endurance.
- **Orrery Academies of Ithra** suggests calculation, prediction, doctrine, and celestial war theory.

## Campaign structure upgrade

The campaign should introduce each faction by making the player sympathize with them before making the player fight them.

1. **The First Empty Cup:** Narethi Silt-Crowns defend a reservoir town without enough water for everyone.
2. **The Red Road March:** Mile-Crown relief columns reopen a road, then refuse to leave.
3. **Court Beneath No Roof:** Kharuun clans flee dying wells and strike a river convoy.
4. **The Spring That Would Not Kneel:** Kharvoden cantons defend neutrality against two hungry empires.
5. **The Gate That Counted Wrong:** Veyr engineers close a gate to save the system and doom a province.
6. **A Map with No People:** Lethrynn hamlets destroy an imperial survey route.
7. **Every Cup Has a Witness:** the Ash-Salt Ledger survives a famine through ration courts and pays the moral price.
8. **Proof of War:** Ithra proves every faction is acting rationally and still cannot prevent the war.

### AI behavior goals

- Mile-Crown AI should build connected safe roads, not random roads.
- Silt-Crowns AI should protect reservoirs and hold water for crisis windows.
- Oathholds AI should choose ground and avoid dumb chases.
- Moving Court AI should raid, feint, and avoid static siege unless it has advantage.
- Sluice Synod AI should prepare engineered zones, then force fights there.
- Veilways AI should use hidden paths and attack support units.
- Ash-Salt AI should avoid wasteful fights and pressure supply.
- Orrery AI should scout, detect repeated player habits, and counter them.

### Balance laws

1. Every exciting faction button must create a future vulnerability.
2. Every strong economy must be attackable through map objectives.
3. Every mobility advantage must have a terrain or scouting counter.
4. Every defensive identity must still have a way to finish total victory.
5. Every advanced faction must have a UI that teaches the player what is happening.
6. No faction should be balanced by making it boring.

## Best launch trio

The best first prototype trio is now:

1. **Auctorate of the Mile-Crown:** baseline combined arms, roads, forts, and conquest legitimacy.
2. **Kharuun Moving Court:** mobility, raids, scouting, and anti-road play.
3. **Sluice Synod of Veyr:** engineers, crossings, siege, and map authorship.

This creates a strong triangle:

- Mile-Crown beats careless Kharuun by fortifying roads and wells.
- Kharuun beats slow Veyr by raiding setup phases.
- Veyr beats predictable Mile-Crown by locking roads and forcing engineered fights.

Add **Narethi Silt-Crowns** fourth to introduce reservoir economy and heroic defensive comeback play.

## Final creative target

The player should never think, “This is the Roman faction, the Mongol faction, the engineer faction.”

The player should think:

- “The Mile-Crown is turning roads into law.”
- “The Silt-Crowns can save everyone if I keep the reservoirs alive.”
- “The Oathholds are unbeatable if I let them choose ground.”
- “The Moving Court is not where my army thinks it is.”
- “The Synod is changing the shape of the war.”
- “The Veilways are fighting from a map I cannot see.”
- “The Ledger can survive decisions that would break me.”
- “The Academies are learning me.”

That is the identity standard for Proteus 218.
